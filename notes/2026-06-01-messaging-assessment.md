## Framing

This assesses every section of the circuit-land marketing page against the bounded-autonomy research, asking one question per line of copy: does this claim match what the research supports, and does it match what Circuit actually does? The honest headline is that the page is already strongly aligned. It leads with repeatable process over autonomy, ties completion to evidence, names the trust gap in the audience's own words, and never claims the agent is a drop-in replacement. So this is mostly a validation pass plus a small number of targeted sharpenings. Where I recommend a change, the existing line is usually good and the edit makes an implicit research point explicit. Only one line on the page is in real tension with the research, and it is the hero subhead.

## Product facts verified

The recommendations below no longer rest on guesswork about what Circuit does. The load-bearing product claims were checked against the Circuit runtime source, and the results let several earlier "pending product-fact check" hedges retire. What was confirmed:

- **The pause is flow-enforced, not agent-offered.** The "human-decision" block is a typed checkpoint (execution kind "checkpoint"), and the runtime engine stops and waits for operator input. The gate is a property of the flow and the harness, not a courtesy the agent chooses to extend. (Circuit runtime: human-decision checkpoint block; checkpoint executor.)
- **Checkpoint-resume is real.** Run state persists keyed by a run id, and the same run resumes from the checkpoint rather than restarting. (Circuit runtime: checkpoint-resume, graph runner.)
- **The run is legible.** Circuit surfaces the chosen flow up front ("Chose build.") and streams per-block progress as each block executes ("Framing the work...", "Planning the work...", and so on). (Circuit runtime: route selection in the CLI; per-block progress strings in the Build flow.)
- **Traces and reports carry residual risk.** Reports include what changed, the checks and verification results, and residual risks and uncertainty. (Circuit runtime: flow report writers, residual-risk fields.)
- **No upfront full-block preview before work starts.** Blocks are revealed progressively as they run; there is no pre-execution reviewable plan preview today. The first block (Frame) is itself a checkpoint that confirms the brief, but that is not a full-plan preview.
- **No rollback or undo.** Fix records a baseline git snapshot for comparison and diff only, not restoration. Circuit ships checkpoint-resume, not rollback.

The practical effect: the flow-enforced pause, checkpoint-resume, the legible run, and residual-risk-bearing traces are confirmed shippable and stated below as facts. The full-block preview "before any work starts" and any rollback or undo claim remain off-limits and stay in Uncertain and Avoid.

## What the page already gets right

This is the validation pass, and it carries most of the weight.

- **"a plugin for Claude Code and Codex"** maps to the workflow-versus-agent boundary. Circuit is the process layer, the host agent is the LLM inside it. The tagline keeps that honest. (Anthropic, "Building Effective Agents" 2024; report Section 4 pattern 1 / Section 6 recommendation 1.)
- **"Powerful, repeatable work patterns for coding agents."** leads with repeatability over speed, which ages better than a speed number. (DORA, report Section 6 recommendation 6: AI raises throughput but degrades stability via large unreviewed batches.)
- **"Plan -> change -> check -> review."** encodes per-stage process with the gates placed after the act step, which is exactly where the research says they belong. (Parasuraman, Sheridan and Wickens 2000, per-stage autonomy, report Sections 1 and 2; Onnasch et al. 2014, advise-to-act boundary.)
- **"A factual, verified result is provided. No gaslighting."** sells reliability over capability and names the dominant failure in the audience's own vocabulary. Section 2 recommends a sharpen of this line that makes "verified" auditable while keeping "No gaslighting"; that is a refinement of an already-strong line, not a sign the current copy is weak. (Stack Overflow 2025: 66% "almost right, but not quite"; report Section 5.)
- **"Each flow is a specialized set of typed blocks. Each block does one job."** is the workflow-versus-agent claim stated plainly, with the precision ("typed", "one job") that makes it structural rather than vibes. (Anthropic, "Building Effective Agents" 2024; report Section 4 pattern 1.)
- **The "Prompting + Skills" comparison card is the page's clearest skills-to-circuits framing.** It draws the one-move-versus-process line directly: a skill gives the agent a stronger move and you still decide which move to call, while Circuit uses those moves inside a flow that routes the task, chooses the next block, carries context forward, and asks for your judgment only when the result depends on it. That is the workflow-versus-agent distinction in product language, and it should stay as written. (Anthropic, "Building Effective Agents" 2024; report Section 4 pattern 1 / Section 6 recommendation 1, workflow-versus-agent.)
- **"Routed, not chosen"** is a two-word statement of automation (a fixed path) versus autonomy (self-directed departure). (Report Section 1.)
- **"Pauses only when judgment changes the result..."** gates at the consequential fork and stays out of the way otherwise, which avoids approval fatigue. (Onnasch et al. 2014; report Section 6 recommendation 3; report Section 8 tension 1.)
- **"Checks behavior, scope, and proof from a separate review posture before the run calls itself done."** is the independent-review channel, the single strongest control in the empirical record. (Gomez 2025: harmful actions ~39% to ~1% via pause plus independent review; report Section 3.)
- **"Circuit keeps checks and results attached to the work... instead of asking you to take it on faith."** sells the audit trail as the trust mechanism, not paperwork. (Report executive summary point 4; Lee and See 2004.)
- **"Circuit is not another agent. It is the process your agent follows..."** is the cleanest expression of the page's most important framing, and it ends on reproducibility, not a capability superlative. (Anthropic, "Building Effective Agents" 2024; report Section 6 recommendation 1, called "the single most important framing for Circuit".)
- **The Memory card is kept future-tense** ("soon" chip, "substrate for longitudinal memory"), so it does not overclaim a capability that is still forthcoming and agent-facing. (Report stat rules: memory is future / labeled soon.)
- **"OpenCode support coming soon."** labels an unfinished capability plainly, which is the candor the research prescribes. (Report Section 7 messaging principles 7 and 10, "be candid".)

## Cross-cutting messaging moves

Page-level moves the research supports, each cited.

1. **Tighter bounds enable more autonomy, not less.** The counterintuitive result: scoping the gate to the moments that matter lets the agent run free everywhere else, with fewer interruptions. The page already does this ("Otherwise it keeps moving"); make the gradient deliberate across sections. (Report Section 7 messaging principle 8; Anthropic sandbox: 84% fewer permission prompts.)
2. **Safe even when the agent is wrong.** Anchor "trust" to the bounds holding even if the agent makes a bad or adversarial choice, never to trusting the agent's competence. (Report Section 7 messaging principle 2; AI Control / Greenblatt 2023.)
3. **Name the checkpoint a guaranteed pause.** The pause is structural and flow-enforced, because corrigibility is unsolved and an agent cannot be relied on to volunteer a stop. This is Circuit's strongest honest differentiator and is currently underclaimed. The report prescribes harness-enforced pause (stop/gate must be harness-enforced and external to the agent), and the Circuit runtime delivers it: the human-decision checkpoint block makes the engine stop and wait for operator input. State it as a confirmed guarantee, no longer pending a product-fact check. (Report Section 7 principle 4; Soares 2014; confirmed against the human-decision checkpoint block and checkpoint executor.)
4. **Speak to the documented trust gap.** The audience uses these tools heavily but trusts them little, and experienced developers are the most skeptical. Lead with reliability and reviewability over raw capability. (Stack Overflow 2025: ~84% use, ~33% trust, ~46% distrust, 66% "almost right, but not quite"; report Section 5 / Section 7 messaging principle 6.)
5. **Evidence is the trust mechanism, not paperwork.** Completion ties to passing checks and a readable trace, not to the agent's claim of done. Confirmed: traces and reports carry what changed, the checks and verification results, and residual risk, so surface those three rather than a bare diff plus green check. Do not assert a blanket per-step "why"; step-level reasoning is captured at decision points only, so keep the claim to changes, checks, and residual risk. (Report executive summary point 4; Lee and See 2004; Parasuraman and Manzey 2010; confirmed against the Circuit report writers.)
6. **Process repeatability, not speed.** The defensible claim is the same work moved the same way every time, because the throughput-for-stability tradeoff means a bounded product cannot promise blanket speedups. (DORA, report Section 6 recommendation 6: AI raises throughput but degrades delivery stability via large unreviewed batches; METR: -19% slower with a ~39-point perception gap.)

The Control card must stay to its two confirmed facts (flow-enforced pause and checkpoint-resume) and must not imply edits can be reverted or that a run halts to a restored safe state.
- **Do not claim an operator stop-anytime primitive or a halt-to-safe-state guarantee distinct from the checkpoint.** Confirmed scope: the runtime exposes a flow-driven pause at the human-decision checkpoint and resume of the same run, not an operator stop-anytime primitive or a halt-to-safe-state guarantee separate from the checkpoint.
